# How has the Stormlight world already been pictured?

Research for issue #3 (child of map #1, "[B solo] The demo: Kaladin's leap and the Second Ideal"). Read and looked at on 2026-11-25. Nothing here is a (B) or (A) position except where tagged. Everything labelled **Fact** comes from the page, book or credit it cites. Everything labelled **Judgement** is mine (the research agent's), not sourced, and not agreed with anyone.

**Why this exists:** the visuals of this world already exist. B's slice question is different: *what does it look like as a game, in one moment?* This note maps the existing ground so slices can build on it and find room for an original take.

**Copyright handling (B's rule, issue #1 Notes and the Dragonsteel ticket):** this file holds links, page references and our own one-line descriptions only. No image was downloaded into the repo, embedded or committed. No book prose is quoted (paraphrase, cited by chapter). Artists are credited by name. Official art is never fed to a generator, and no living artist's name goes into a generation prompt (names appear in this file so we can find and credit work, not to be prompted).

## Short answer

1. **B's main inspiration is also an official insider.** `lamaery_in_the_cosmere` is the Instagram account of **Marie Seeberger** (alias LaMaery), a German illustrator (Coppermind's artist page, which links that account). The Cosmere RPG books credit "Marie Seeberger" as **Concept Artist** on every book I checked. Her "Dulac's travelogue" sketchbook pages are published in the RPG's *Stormlight World Guide* (e.g. PDF pp. 161, 171). So the (B) inspiration and the official RPG look overlap, and the overlap is her notebook-and-sketch register more than the glossy painted register.
2. **The official look has two registers.** (a) A bright, clean, painterly-realist illustration look (the RPG's full-bleed plates and character cards, dozens of credited painters). (b) A "field notebook" look (in-world sketchbooks by Shallan, Navani and the in-world traveller Dulac: Isaac Stewart, Ben McSweeney, Marie Seeberger).
3. **The exact demo beat has already been pictured by fans**, at least three ways (Marie Seeberger, Grant M. Hansen, Jessica Liu), and they disagree sharply: bright and airy vs. dark silhouette vs. quiet and intimate. That disagreement is the open space.
4. **Nearly everything found is an illustration composition** (side-on, mid-air, poster framing). I found no game-frame depiction of the leap. Game-like fan work that exists is small: a Kaladin-movement prototype, an unreleased Szeth metroidvania, an Unreal Engine 5 animated short (see "Game-like work").
5. **Not reached:** Instagram captions and posts beyond the 12 visible in the logged-out grid; most of the RPG PDFs' pages beyond the ones listed (see "Limits").

## How to read the citations

- **RPG pages are PDF page numbers** (the number in a PDF viewer). In the *World Guide* and *Handbook* the printed page number is 4 lower than the PDF page. The other books match.
- **RPG artist credits are tiny vertical text in the gutter,** so the credit can sit beside the art on the same page or on the facing page. Where I matched a credit to an image by position only, I say **(credit by position)**. Where a page had no credit in the text layer I say **(credit not located)**; the book's credits page lists every illustrator but doesn't map them to pages.
- **Coppermind** (coppermind.net) credits each file page with artist, book and source. Its licence is CC BY-NC-ND: link, don't derive. Coppermind pages are a lookup, not the canon; canon claims below cite the books, and the book text was read firsthand from the off-repo reference library (see `docs/agents/reference-library.md`).
- **Art direction of the RPG books** (credits pages): Art Director Katie Payne (Matthew DeMino on the later booklets); Graphic Designer Gordon McAlpin; Concept Artist Marie Seeberger; producers Isaac Stewart and Dan Wells; Dragonsteel Art Team: Ben McSweeney, Shawn Boyles, Rachael Lynn Buchanan, Anna Earley, Hayley Lazo, Jennifer Neal, Priscilla Spencer. Cover illustrators: Ekaterina Burmak (*Handbook*, *World Guide*), Amirul Hhf (*Welcome to Roshar*, *Bridge Nine*), Kevin O'Neill (*The First Step*), Petar Penev (*Stonewalkers*).

## 1. lamaery_in_the_cosmere (B's main inspiration)

- **Who:** Marie Seeberger, alias LaMaery. Her Coppermind artist page lists Instagram `lamaery_in_the_cosmere`, Tumblr `lamaery`, Behance `marieseeberger`, DeviantArt `palila`. It calls her a German illustrator who does fan art for the freedom it gives from her commissioned work. Instagram's logged-out profile shows about 21.6K followers, and a bio line pointing to a separate non-Cosmere account `@seeberger.illustration` and a print shop. Links: [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/lamaery_in_the_cosmere/), [Coppermind artist page](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Coppermind:Artists/Marie_Seeberger), [Tumblr](https://lamaery.tumblr.com/), [Behance](https://www.behance.net/marieseeberger), [INPRNT shop](https://www.inprnt.com/gallery/lamaery/).
- **Official credits (Fact):** "Concept Artist: Marie Seeberger" on the credits page of the *Handbook*, *World Guide*, *Welcome to Roshar*, *The First Step*, *Bridge Nine* and *Stonewalkers*. Coppermind lists her official artwork as "King Lopen the First of Alethkar" and "Dulac's travelogue" (Rall Elorim, an Alethi warcamp, the Shadesmar city Celebrant, others). Her credit is printed on *World Guide* p. 162, next to the Rall Elorim sketchbook spread on p. 161, and (by position) next to the warcamp sketchbook spread on p. 171; it also appears on p. 201, which I did not look at.

The Leap piece (below) is the bright end of her range: sky-blue and ochre, flat painterly shapes.

## 3. By subject: what is settled, where it diverges, references

Format: **Settled** (canon plus consensus across what I saw), **Open** (where depictions diverge or the books leave room), **References** (3–6, with credit).

### Syl (and spren)

- **Settled (Fact, book):** She first appears as a windspren, a ribbon of light with no fixed form (*Way of Kings* ch. 2, "Honor Is Dead"). In the Physical Realm she can take several shapes; Coppermind's page records ribbon, leaves, flame and small cloud, but her preferred shape is a young woman about a handspan tall, angular face, hair that fades to mist, a girlish dress, always barefoot, everything blue-white and faintly glowing, usually visible only to Kaladin and Rock.
- **Settled by consensus (Judgement):** blue-white palette, translucent, tiny humanoid with a long skirt and flowing hair. The RPG sticks to the same: a tiny figure or a ribbon.
- **Open:** (1) ribbon vs figure vs flame: depictions pick one; the RPG itself shows both (*Handbook* p. 133 ribbon, *World Guide* p. 132 tiny figure). (2) How solid she looks: *Handbook* p. 209 renders honorspren as ghostly full-size people in clothes; fan art often keeps her small. (3) Scale and attitude are authorial: playful, mischievous in ch. 2, protective later; the faces vary widely.
- **References:**

### Stormlight and Lashings

- **Settled (Fact):** In the book's key scene Kaladin breathes in Stormlight that bursts from the gemstones woven into the Parshendi's beards and streams toward him (*Way of Kings* ch. 67, "Words"). The RPG describes Stormlight as behaving like luminescent white smoke, infusing gems (*World Guide* p. 13 text). Lashings change which way "down" is: the RPG's Windrunner art shows flight, wall-running and gravity shifts without a physical "cause" beyond light.
- **Settled by consensus (Judgement):** a blue-white glow on the body and a **trail** behind it; a rising smoke-like wisp when released; a ring or swirl of light at the points where the "pull" acts. Violet or blue lightning accompanies highstorms; red lightning is the Everstorm.
- **Open:** (1) smoke vs glow vs ribbons vs sparks: official art uses all of them (*Handbook* p. 203 swirls, p. 133 vapour, *World Guide* p. 8 rings). (2) How a Lashing reads in motion is **unsolved**: every depiction here is a still, and the visuals that exist for it are trails and rings. (3) The moment of *draining* Stormlight from the gems (the ch. 67 action) is barely pictured. The fan versions below draw the glow already on Kaladin.
- **References:**
  1. *World Guide* p. 8, a Radiant above a chasm with blue ring-swirls at the feet. Anthony Avon (by position).

### The Shattered Plains and the chasms

- **Settled (Fact, book):** A broken platter: plateaus separated by chasms twenty to thirty feet wide, so deep they vanish in darkness (*Way of Kings* ch. 4, "The Shattered Plains"). Isaac Stewart's official map (Coppermind, "Map of the Shattered Plains") shows the symmetric overhead pattern; the RPG adds that the plateau pattern radiates from a ruined city, the warcamps sit in ten collapsed domes on the western edge, and the chasms are wet and alive (*World Guide* pp. 162–168, text). The Tower is the large plateau at the lower right of the *Way of Kings* map.
- **Settled by consensus (Judgement):** orange and rust stone, flat-topped plateaus, a sharp vertical cut for the chasm edge. The RPG's *World Guide* p. 8, *Bridge Nine* p. 10 and 15, and Nick van Kleef's plate agree on the colour.
- **Open:** (1) the chasm interior: sometimes a dark crack, sometimes a green wet canyon with plants. (2) Scale: fan art ranges from intimate ledges to aerial vistas. (3) Sky: warm sunset, hard blue, or storm; the leap pieces below pick differently. (4) The **top-down** view (the map's symmetry) is almost never used in painted art, which is a gap relevant to a game camera.

### Bridge crews and bridges

- **Settled (Fact, book):** Crews carry large wooden bridges to the edge of a plateau and push or lay them across the chasm; that is the setting for the demo, where the bridge is still moving when Kaladin jumps (see the [beat sheet](https://github.com/beejsbj/stormlight-game/blob/research/tower-battle-beats/docs/sources/2026-11-25-tower-battle-beats.md) for the chapter-by-chapter detail). Bridge Four has its own glyph and tattoo marks (Isaac Stewart, Coppermind). Official RPG art shows the bridge crew in Kholin blue after the book's turn, and the *Bridge Nine* adventure shows a bridge crew with a chull caravan (p. 6).
- **Settled by consensus (Judgement):** a heavy plain wooden bridge with a side frame, a long crew of people under and behind it, dusty cloth. The brown-and-tan palette of the "bridgeman" look is quite consistent.
- **Open:** (1) bridge construction: the RPG shows a plank bridge hauled by a chull caravan; Seeberger's Leap piece shows a framed truss bridge being pushed. (2) Crew uniforms **after** their freedom (the blue Kholin uniform) are well fixed by the RPG, but **before** (leather vests, bare shoulders, the slave brand) vary. (3) Almost no art shows the bridge **mid-run from the carriers' side** under the bridge, i.e. the view a game would most likely use.

### Parshendi (listeners)

- **Settled (Fact, book):** Marbled skin in red and black, with white in some, unique to each person; carapace-style armour; the ch. 67 passage describes carapace breastplates and skullcaps and **beards woven with glowing gemstones** (*Way of Kings* ch. 67; skin, build and armour also in ch. 3 "City of Bells" and ch. 6 "Bridge Four"). The RPG names the people "listeners" and shows warform and nimbleform side by side (*Welcome to Roshar* p. 24, with callouts for painted arrow fletching, gems woven into clothes and beards, carapace armour).
- **Settled by consensus (Judgement):** red-and-black marbling, a stocky warform with a heavy plated chest and a rangy nimbleform; red-orange carapace.
- **Open:** (1) How much of the gems-in-beards detail survives into art: the RPG callouts include it; most fan pieces don't. (2) The marbling: a dense camouflage pattern vs bold clean patches. (3) Whether they look **menacing** (RPG melee plates) or **human** (Seeberger's portraits of individuals), a real choice for a demo that asks the player to leap into them.
- **References:**
  1. *Welcome to Roshar* p. 24, warform and nimbleform with callouts; "Parshendi warriors surround an Alethi Shardbearer". Credit not located.

### Shardbearers and Shardplate

- **Settled (Fact):** Plate is full armour that makes the bearer fast and strong, with glowing, cracked seams that leak Stormlight when damaged (RPG: *Welcome to Roshar* p. 5, *World Guide* text); Blades are long, ornate, silvery and summoned. The stances scroll (*Handbook* p. 123, and Isaac Stewart's in-world "scroll of stances") gives Plate a fighting pose vocabulary.
- **Settled by consensus (Judgement):** stylised helmed full armour with etched glyph-lines, a large blade, bright blue Kholin-style Plate for the main characters, order-specific colours for Radiant Plate.
- **Open:** (1) Plate colours and glyph styling are **not** fixed: Steve Argyle's Radiant-order series and the RPG's Sonderflex Windrunner plate (*Handbook* p. 206) disagree in silhouette. (2) A Blade: longsword vs a big slender fantasy sword; see *Welcome to Roshar* p. 5 vs Coppermind's [Blade designs](https://coppermind.net/wiki/File:Broken_Oath_by_Antti_Hakosaari.jpg). (3) The Plate in motion (a **leap** in Plate) is rare: the closest is the Penev "leaping into battle" plate below.

### Highstorms

- **Settled (Fact):** a hurricane-force storm sweeping the continent from east to west about once per five-day week, throwing boulders ahead of its stormwall, and the thing that recharges Stormlight (RPG *Welcome to Roshar* p. 4 and *World Guide* p. 13 text). The red-lightning Everstorm is a separate, later storm (*World Guide* p. 13).
- **Settled by consensus (Judgement):** blue or violet lightning for the highstorm (red for the Everstorm); a towering churn of cloud with a hard leading edge, lit from within. The "figure on a ledge watching it come" composition recurs in the RPG (*Welcome to Roshar* p. 4, *World Guide* pp. 184–185) and in the leatherbound endpaper.
- **Open:** (1) lightning colour and density: violet, white-blue or red; (2) the storm's front as a literal **wall** (a stormwall) vs a cloud; (3) the highstorm **from inside a chasm** (rising water) is mostly drawn as a quiet blue scene. For the demo, the Tower battle is not a highstorm scene; storm art matters for mood and for Stormlight, not the beat itself.

## 7. Room for an original take (Judgement, labelled mine)

These are observations for B and Ashar to react to, not recommendations. I'm raising them because B's question is "as a game, in one moment".

1. **Almost every picture is an illustration composition** (side view, mid-air hero, wide landscape). I found none that shows the leap from a game-like camera: over the shoulder behind Kaladin, from under the bridge, top-down on the plateau pattern. That is an angle gap, not a style gap.
2. **The look splits in two,** and the official work already holds both: clean painterly-realist plates, and sketchbook/notebook pages. Seeberger's own range spans both. A slice could pick one and say so.
3. **Where the books give less,** depictions diverge most: how Stormlight looks while being **drawn in** (rather than worn), how a Lashing reads in motion, Syl as ribbon vs figure, Plate glyph styling, and whether the listeners look human or monstrous. These are the places where our own description (not a copied look) matters most.
4. **Where copying would be most visible:** the Bridge Four glyph and tattoo, Windrunner and Radiant-order emblems, the specific blue Kholin uniform cut and the particular Plate designs. These are Dragonsteel's marks and designs; "evoking" them needs a recorded departure (an ADR if it's an intentional departure from the books).
5. **The overlap in (1) of the short answer** is worth a conscious decision: B's main inspiration shaped the official RPG's notebook look. "Evoke, don't copy" is harder when the inspiration is also the licensed reference. A slice that records "what we took and what we changed" (B's process rule) covers this.
