# Dragonsteel's fan-content rules, and what they allow for AI-made fan art

Research for issue #6, "What do Dragonsteel's fan-content rules allow for AI-made fan art?" (child of map #1, a solo (B) map). Read 2026-11-25. Everything labelled **Fact** is from the owning party's own page, cited inline. Everything labelled **Judgement** is mine (the agent's), not sourced, and not agreed with anyone.

**Not legal advice.** I'm not a lawyer; this reports what the published policies say and reasons from them. Policies change (the main one was last updated 2026-08-20); re-read the live page before relying on any line here.

**How the quotes were obtained.** The pages were read through a fetch tool that limits each quote to about 125 characters, so most clauses below are short quoted fragments with my paraphrase around them, flagged as such. Quoted fragments are verbatim as returned; check the live page for full context before quoting them anywhere that matters.

## Short answer

- **Fact:** Dragonsteel's fan art policy welcomes *non-commercial* fan art, and names posting to Reddit/Instagram/Tumblr as an example. The one AI clause in the policy bars AI-made work for **commercial** uses only. The policy says nothing about non-commercial AI-made art, either way.
- **Fact:** Visual fan art, commercial or not, must be *original*: it may depict characters, scenes and places from the books but may not include a copy of Dragonsteel's own art (covers, interior art, maps, symbols). Copying by hand is still copying.
- **Fact:** There is **no category for fan games** in Dragonsteel's fan art policy, and the policy says digital-gaming licensing inquiries are turned down. It also says **Brotherwise Games holds the exclusive right to make games based on the Stormlight Archive.** Brotherwise's Cosmere RPG fan-content policy covers *tabletop* game content (rules, maps, adventures), not video games.
- **Fact:** Brandon Sanderson has a strong public stance *against* AI art (Jan 2026 keynote), but the keynote is an essay about art and process. It says nothing about fan art or fan content, and it isn't policy.
- **Judgement:** AI-generated concept slices in a public repo look inside what's published, if they are original, unmistakably unofficial, and earn nothing. A free fan *video game* looks outside everything that's published; assume it needs written permission. "Ask whether it could ever be sold" runs into three published closed doors (see section 5).

## 1. What each source says

### 1a. Dragonsteel fan art policy (the governing text)

- URL: <https://www.brandonsanderson.com/pages/fan-art-policy>, titled "Fan Art, Fan Film, and Licensing Policies". Line at the foot of the page: "Last Updated: August 20, 2026."
- Older copies live on the FAQ ([fan art FAQ](https://faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/can-i-make-fan-art-or-write-fan-fiction), "Last updated 7-10-2023", with a banner saying the policy has moved to the page above). Treat the brandonsanderson.com page as current.
- The page has two parallel sections, a "Layman's Terms" one and a "Legal" one. Where they differ, I note it.

**Non-commercial fan art: welcome, with limits**
- Fact: "I have no objection to Fan Art and Fan Fiction created solely for non-commercial personal use," "so long as you are not making any money from them directly or indirectly." Examples: "posting your Fan Art to Reddit, Instagram, Tumblr, etc."
- Fact: "'Commercial' means anything sold for monetary compensation, or used to collect monetary compensation in any way." Test given: "Am I selling it? Or gaining a monetary profit from it in any way?"
- Fact (Legal, Visual Art): allowed non-commercial uses of visual fan art ("no direct or indirect compensation, financial or otherwise") include "electronic distribution".
- Fact: the default is closed: "copies, derivative works, distributions, and public displays are not allowed" unless a section permits them; and "Dragonsteel DOES NOT permit public displays of any text or art from any of the Dragonsteel Works." So book text and official art stay out of the public repo.

**Originality and official art**
- Fact (Legal, Visual Art): fan art "is limited to original works depicting characters, scenes, geographies, and/or other elements from Dragonsteel Works" and "must not include or incorporate a whole, partial, or substantial copy of any visual art from any Dragonsteel Works." "Copying by hand or other non-automated means is still copying."
- Fact (Layman's): "you can't copy visual artwork from my books such as book covers, interior art, symbols, and maps." (Also notes that maps of places without official maps are treated like copies.)
- Fact: the word "reference" does not appear on the page. Nothing says whether official art may be used as a *reference* for original work. The nearest text is the ban on a "whole, partial, or substantial copy" and on "a manual/hand reproduction of distinctive artistic elements". The page says that Dragonsteel will not answer fair-use questions and grants no permissions beyond the page (paraphrase of the "Notes" list).
- Judgement: "looking at official art to inform an original piece" is not covered; "tracing, img2img or uploading official art as an input to a generator" would be copying under any reading of "partial or substantial copy", and "non-automated means" in the sentence above suggests automated copying is *also* meant to be covered. Don't feed official art to a generator.

**The AI clause**
- Fact (Layman's, under the heading "NOT ALLOWED COMMERCIAL FAN ART USES"): fan art "created by or with the aid of AI (Artificial Intelligence) software (e.g., ChatGPT, Midjourney, Suno, etc.)" "is not permissible for any commercial uses."
- Fact (Legal, under "Non-allowable Fan Art/Fiction", whose lead-in says the listed items "ARE NEVER permitted"): "created by or with the aid of AI (Artificial Intelligence) software (e.g., ChatGPT, Midjourney, Dall-E, etc.)" "is not permissible for any commercial uses."
- Fact: both versions are limited by their own words to "any commercial uses". The page does not mention non-commercial AI-made art. "Machine" does not appear.
- Judgement: the more natural reading is "commercial AI art: never; non-commercial AI art: unaddressed, so governed by the general non-commercial permission". The Legal section's "NEVER permitted" lead-in plus an unaddressed case leaves a gap that someone could read the other way, and the policy also says to assume something is not permitted if unsure (paraphrase of the Legal section). That's a real uncertainty, not a settled "yes".

**Disclaimers and credit**
- Fact: no credit requirement for fan art.
- Fact (Legal, "Note re Context"): "The context, presentation, title, and/or written description for any Fan Art must make it clear that the Fan Art is unofficial." Layman's offers one example: "fan-created, inspired by Brandon Sanderson." No fixed wording is required for art.
- Fact: fan fiction (non-commercial only) needs "a conspicuous notification that it is unofficial fan fiction".
- Fact: claims of being "official" "MUST BE IN WRITING and is otherwise invalid"; using text or art "to suggest that a business, blog, social media account, etc. is" "official" or endorsed is not allowed.

**Money-adjacent rules that touch a repo**
- Fact: crowdfunding is never allowed for fan art, fan fiction, fan books, fanzines or other fan projects (Kickstarter, IndieGoGo, Patreon named; "consistent or regular compensation for Fan Art using these platforms is not allowed").
- Fact: fan fiction earning "advertising revenue" "IS NEVER permitted".
- Judgement: for a repo, the practical read is: no sponsor buttons, ads, paid tiers or donation links on anything showing slices.

**Games and licensing**
- Fact: "Dragonsteel turns down music and digital gaming inquiries because those rights are always closely tied to film/tv rights." Dragonsteel "generally does not take unsolicited licensing inquiries" (paraphrase). Examples of licensed work: "Brotherwise Games has the exclusive right to make games based on the Stormlight Archive." and "Nauvoo Games has the right to make games based on the Reckoners."
- Fact: "Fan Films" section: film or movie adaptations of any Dragonsteel or Sanderson work are not allowed "at this time, in any length, media, or technology" (paraphrase; stated reasons are the legacy of the written works and possible future development).
- Fact: questions go to legal@dragonsteel.com; unsure means assume not permitted. Permission must be signed and in writing; a conversational "Yeah, that sounds cool" "is not permission to move forward" (paraphrase of the "Notes" list).
- Fact: the policy's visual-art section says nothing about game stills or UI mockups specifically.

### 1b. Brotherwise Games: Cosmere RPG fan content policy

- URL: <https://www.cosmererpg.com/fan-content-policy> (no date on page). Community page (<https://www.cosmererpg.com/community>) summarises the same policy more loosely ("shared with the community for free"; "does not infringe on copyrighted works"). The policy page is the fuller statement.
- Fact: Brotherwise and Dragonsteel "encourage fan content for the Cosmere RPG, as long as it is non-commercial" and "contains the correct attributions/disclaimers" and "does not contain any copyrighted artwork or logos." Examples are game content: new rules, maps, adventures, a heroic path specialty; plus "actual plays or reviews".
- Fact: "Fanmade Cosmere RPG game content should not be monetized or paywalled in any way." Actual plays and streams may be monetized (paraphrase).
- Fact, required disclaimer in any game content shared: "This is unofficial fan content, created and shared for non-commercial use." and "It has not been reviewed by Dragonsteel Entertainment, LLC or Brotherwise Games, LLC." (Paraphrase: the exact surrounding sentence structure isn't reproduced here; take the wording from the page.)
- Fact: don't copy "the page backgrounds, border elements, and cover templates of official content"; those are "copyrighted 'trade dress'". The page lets fans use the Opportunity, Complication, action and reaction icons.
- Fact: AI, video games and digital products, third-party tools: **not mentioned** on the page. The page points fan art to the Dragonsteel policy above.
- Fact: Brotherwise "intends to share an OGL (Open Gaming License) version of the underlying ruleset" (Plotweaver) "which removes any references to Dragonsteel IP", allowing monetization "but not of any content related to or resembling Dragonsteel-owned IP". Future plan, not yet a licence.
- Fact: a file list on the page includes an "official fan content template" Word zip (not fetched).
- Judgement: this policy is about the tabletop Cosmere RPG's system and content. A Stormlight *video game* is not game content for that RPG, so this policy doesn't cover it, even though its spirit (free, unofficial, disclaimer) is the closest published template.

### 1c. Brandon Sanderson on AI art

- Source: ["The Hidden Cost of AI Art" keynote](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/ai-art-brandon-sanderson-keynote), posted January 30, 2026 (byline date); the speech was given at a Dragonsteel Nexus release event.
- Fact: his core position, in his words: "I think this is why I rebel against AI art products so much: because they steal the opportunity for growth from us." and "Using those models in such a way absolutely misses the point, because it looks at art only as a product." He says he does not mainly dislike AI because its art is poorly done.
- Fact: the keynote does **not** mention fan art, fan content, Dragonsteel's policy on fans' AI use, or licensing for AI-made works. (Per the fetch of the full page.)
- Judgement: this is the culture the policy sits in. The commercial-only clause is a rule; his speech is a stance. The speech suggests that a *non-commercial* AI fan work, though technically unaddressed, is the kind of thing he dislikes. That is a reputational consideration, not a legal one, and it's yours to weigh.

## 2. What isn't established (read before relying)

- **No AI-specific policy** from Dragonsteel beyond the commercial clause, and **none from Brotherwise** (the Cosmere RPG fan-content page is silent on AI). I did not find any Dragonsteel or Brotherwise statement on AI in fan content, nor on AI training on official art.
- **Fan video games:** no published permission and no published prohibition in those words. My read is the default-closed rule plus the exclusive Stormlight games grant.
- **A 2024 Kickstarter FAQ** for the Cosmere RPG reportedly said fan art and fan fiction are allowed but fan games are not under the then-current policy (seen as a search-result snippet only; the BackerKit FAQ page returned 403 to the fetch tool and the Kickstarter page was not read). Treat as unverified; the policy page above supersedes it.
- The "Last Updated: August 20, 2026" date and the whole policy were read once through a summarising fetch tool; I did not cross-check against a second fetch or an archived copy.
- **Not read:** the Termageddon-hosted Terms of Service on dragonsteelbooks.com (general site terms, unrelated to fan content as far as I saw), the Cosmere RPG fan content Word template, and the Brandon Sanderson YouTube/podcast statements on AI beyond the keynote.

## 3. What this implies

### (a) AI slices in the public repo

- **Fact basis:** non-commercial electronic distribution of original visual fan art is permitted; the AI ban is commercial-only; public display of official text/art is not permitted.
- **Judgement, probably fine if all of these hold:**
  1. Slices are original depictions of Stormlight characters/scenes/places, with no official art as input (no img2img, no uploading covers, interior art, maps, RPG art as references to the generator).
  2. The repo, README and each slice make clear the work is **unofficial**, with a short fan disclaimer (policy gives example wording "fan-created, inspired by Brandon Sanderson"; Brotherwise's RPG wording is a good model).
  3. **No money, direct or indirect:** no sponsor links, ads, paid tiers, Patreon, crowdfunding, or "support the project" buttons around it, and nothing that functions as a portfolio sale. A repo that is later used to fund-raise would flip the analysis.
  4. No Dragonsteel names in anything resembling a storefront or product title, and no claims to be official.
- **Tension with the map's note:** the map (#1) says "Use links and short cited quotes only" for book prose. The policy's "no public displays of any text" is the default, and fan-fiction text copying appears to be allowed only as short excerpts tied to allowed visual art (paraphrase; I'm not certain of this reading). **Mine, not B's:** worth a deliberate choice about how much quoted prose the public repo carries, e.g. the beat sheet (#2). I haven't audited the existing files.
- **Tool terms are separate:** the generator's own terms still apply (#4 found Midjourney public-by-default, Leonardo free-plan ownership, Canva's C2PA rule). Those are not Dragonsteel's rules but matter for a public repo.
- **The residual risk is reading, not text:** the policy doesn't address non-commercial AI art, and Sanderson publicly dislikes it. Asking `legal@dragonsteel.com` is the only way to turn "unaddressed" into "answered"; the policy says to assume no when unsure.

### (b) A free fan game

- **Fact basis:** no fan-game category in the Dragonsteel policy; default is no derivative works; digital-gaming licensing inquiries refused; Brotherwise exclusively licensed for Stormlight games; Cosmere RPG fan-content policy is tabletop; fan films banned in any technology.
- **Judgement:** a free, public, playable Stormlight video game isn't covered by any published permission. Treat it as *needing written permission* from Dragonsteel (and given the exclusivity, possibly Brotherwise's blessing), not as something you can ship on the strength of the fan art policy. A private prototype for the two designers is outside what these policies address (they're about public distribution). A public playable build, a trailer with game footage, or a cutscene-heavy demo is where the "games" and "fan films" language bites. I'd not read "free" as a safe harbour: the policy's test is about monetary compensation, not whether the thing is a game.
- **Options (attributed where they come from; labelled mine):**
  1. Keep the demo as *private prototypes and public slices/bible only* (consistent with the map's current framing: slices are thinking tools). (Mine, matching the map's Destination.)
  2. Email `legal@dragonsteel.com` asking whether a free, non-commercial fan prototype/demo is acceptable, with the AI-use question included. (Mine; the contact is the policy's own.)
  3. Contact Brotherwise. (The only address in the fetched fan-content page is for RPG video content questions, `jillian@brotherwisegames.com`; I'm not sure that's the right route for a video game.)

### (c) "Ask Brandon whether it could ever be sold"

- **Fact:** (i) Dragonsteel "generally does not take unsolicited licensing inquiries" and turns down digital-gaming inquiries; (ii) Brotherwise has the exclusive Stormlight games right; (iii) the policy's commercial-use rules ban AI-made work outright and ban crowdfunding for fan projects; (iv) only signed written permission counts, and an enthusiastic remark from Brandon doesn't.
- The FAQ's "Licensing Inquiries" entry (dated 7-10-2023, so possibly stale) routes product-licensing inquiries to an agent at JABberwocky Literary Agency, and says to get a licence before investing in design or manufacturing.
- **Judgement:** the published channels close this path three ways at once: no game licensing, an existing exclusive licensee, and a policy that forbids AI-made work in commercial uses. Asking Brandon is not forbidden (legal@dragonsteel.com is the stated route), but I'd expect "no" from the published position, and a "yes" in conversation would be worth nothing without signed paper. The realistic route to a sellable game is the one the Cosmere RPG policy hints at: an *original* world, with Dragonsteel IP removed (Brotherwise's planned OGL does exactly that for its rules), which the slices could inform but not be.
- B's decision: whether to send the question at all, and whether the slices should be designed so they could later be re-skinned away from Stormlight. I'm not recommending either.
