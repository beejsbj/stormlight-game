# The Tower battle, beat by beat (*The Way of Kings* chs. 65–68)

Book prose is paraphrased per the repo's no-quotes rule (Dragonsteel fan policy); only the Ideals are quoted.

Research for ticket #2, under the map #1 "[B solo] The demo: Kaladin's leap and the Second Ideal". Nothing here is a (B) or (A) position. It is a reading of the book, plus labelled suggestions from the research agent (me) at the end.

**Source:** the book text, read firsthand from the off-repo reference library (see `docs/agents/reference-library.md`): chs. 65 "The Tower", 66 "Codes", 67 "Words", 68 "Eshonai", with ch. 59 and chs. 63–64 for the run-up, and *Words of Radiance* chs. 84 and 86 for one lore check. Everything is paraphrase, cited by chapter; the only book words quoted are the Ideals'. Coppermind was not needed.

## The shape of the battle

- **Place:** the Tower, the biggest plateau on the Shattered Plains. It is a wedge that tilts down to the west, and only three plateaus on that side can stage an assault (ch. 65). Between plateaus are very wide chasms. Sadeas's bridge crews carry **forty bridges** (ch. 65).
- **Plan:** Sadeas and Dalinar attack together, Sadeas's crews landing first, to trap ~10,000 Parshendi against the cliff and break the war (chs. 64–65).
- **The trap:** a second Parshendi army arrives, Sadeas withdraws with every bridge, and Dalinar's ~8,000 are cut off (chs. 65–66).
- **Bridge Four's turn:** Kaladin, free to flee, goes back (ch. 67).
- **Outcome:** Dalinar's army breaks out to the western chasm and crosses on Bridge Four's bridge. Dalinar and Kaladin meet. Dalinar offers protection and vows it (ch. 68).

POV runs in short alternating blocks. Dalinar, Kaladin, Adolin and Teft each get sections.

## Run-up (before ch. 65, for context only)

- **Ch. 59 "An Honor":**
  - Kaladin first breathes in Stormlight from a sphere and tries to run up a chasm wall. He bounces off and lands hard.
  - He speaks the **First Ideal** (life before death, strength before weakness, journey before destination), after Teft has recited it.
  - He sticks stones to a wall with Stormlight and climbs. Syl talks him into cutting the rope and dropping forty feet, and he lands softly. It is his first controlled fall.
- **Ch. 63 "Fear":** Bridge Four gets carapace armor, trains with spears, and Kaladin and Rock start to plan an escape that fakes the crew's death.
- **Ch. 64 "A Man of Extremes":**
  - Bridge Four marches out in armor, to cheers from other crews, and Kaladin tells his men they are the army's champions.
  - Six bridge runs without a death. Adolin decides to trust his father's visions.
  - Sadeas pitches the joint Tower assault.

## Beat sheet

Slice-potential tags: **E** emotional beat, **S** spectacle, **P** playable, **C** cinematic.

### Ch. 65 "The Tower"

**1. Overlook (Dalinar POV).**
- *Present:* Dalinar, Sadeas, and Adolin nearby.
- *Place:* a rock formation looking over the Tower from a staging plateau.
- *Stakes:* Dalinar is worried about holding firm on his principles. He refuses to make bridgemen carry for him, and says he will attack only after Sadeas has made a landing. He holds to the principle that a commander should not make his men do what he would not do himself.
- *Kaladin:* absent.
- *Slice:* **S/C**. The scale is the point: tens of thousands in blue and red on adjacent plateaus, a wedge-shaped plateau, forty bridges. It is a good establishing shot of what the Shattered Plains look like as a game space. **E**: the Dalinar–Sadeas handshake is the setup for the betrayal.

**2. The decoy dance (Kaladin POV).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, four decoy bridgemen, the rest of Bridge Four behind in carapace armor, Parshendi archers, Sadeas's army surging past.
- *Place:* the western staging plateau before the Tower chasm.
- *Stakes:* The archers focus on Kaladin as a symbol, a banner they want to bring down. Arrows must come close enough to feel real, and he must not draw so much Stormlight that soldiers see.
- *Kaladin's powers:* the chapter opens by describing his dodging as a dance with the wind. Stormlight is pulsing in him and arrows are slapped away with a shield. He balances drawing enough to stay alive against too much to be visible. No named Surge appears.
- *Slice:* **P/S**. This is the book's best rhythmic dodge scene: reading arrows, spinning through them, a limited resource (Stormlight) that must stay hidden. **C** also works.

**3. Field surgery (Kaladin POV).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, Skar (arrow in the foot), Teft (arrow in the shoulder, hiding it), Moash, Lopen, Drehy, Rock.
- *Place:* behind the line, on the staging plateau.
- *Stakes:* the first casualties after six clean runs. Kaladin has to cauterise Teft, apologising as he works.
- *Kaladin:* medical skill, not powers. He has Stormlight to stave off exhaustion.
- *Slice:* **E/P**. A small, quiet beat that shows who Bridge Four are. Playable as a triage or care mechanic.

**4. Dalinar breaks the line (Dalinar POV).**
- *Present:* Dalinar, Adolin, Cobalt Guard, Parshendi slingers and swordsmen.
- *Place:* the Tower slope.
- *Stakes:* it feels like winning again, and he recognises the Thrill.
- *Slice:* **S/P**. A Shardbearer rampage with Plate, slings that crack his vambrace, leaps up onto ledges and a throw of boulders. A good "power fantasy" contrast to Kaladin's. This is the book's pre-betrayal peak.

**5. The Parshendi youth (Dalinar POV).**
- *Present:* Dalinar, a young Parshendi on the ground, dying Parshendi humming the death song, Cobalt Guard.
- *Place:* the battlefield, behind the broken line.
- *Stakes:* he freezes with his blade raised and the Thrill drains from him. A guardsman kills the boy before he can stop it. He recites the Codes and starts doubting his visions (the command in them to unite the highprinces).
- *Slice:* **E**. A quiet moral beat in the middle of a battle. How the death songs sound is itself a bible question.

**6. The reveal (Dalinar POV).**
- *Present:* Dalinar, Adolin, a second Parshendi army, Sadeas's retreating army.
- *Place:* the top of a rock formation on the Tower slope.
- *Stakes:* Sadeas is leaving, and taking his bridges.
- *Slice:* **C**. A single wide shot works best: the realisation seen from above. (Kaladin sees it from the other side in beat 7.)

### Ch. 66 "Codes"

**7. Sadeas retreats (Kaladin POV).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, Moash, Matal, Sadeas on horseback, honor guard, the eight lent bridge crews.
- *Place:* the staging plateau and bridge ten.
- *Stakes:* Kaladin elbows to the bridge to see Sadeas close up, then understands the trap. Sadeas looks back at the Tower and says, softly, as if to Dalinar, that his honor would get him killed someday.
- *Kaladin:* just watching. He is tired from drawing Stormlight.
- *Slice:* **E/C**. This is the betrayal from the bottom of the ladder. It sets up why Kaladin might owe Dalinar nothing, and is the hinge for beat 10.

**8. Dalinar and Adolin accept death (Dalinar POV).**
- *Present:* Dalinar, Adolin, Cobalt Guard, reserves.
- *Place:* an upper slope of the Tower, ringed by Parshendi.
- *Stakes:* Dalinar expects Adolin to blame him. Adolin says it is not his fault and will not have him become like Sadeas. Dalinar then gives a short speech to the men: the end is the same for everyone, and what sets men apart is the path they take to it. He thinks goodbye to Renarin.
- *Slice:* **E**. A father-son beat and a rally speech. Its theme (the Codes, doing the right thing when losing) is the mirror of Kaladin's coming choice.

### Ch. 67 "Words"

**9. Despair on the staging plateau (Kaladin POV).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, Bridge Four (with Teft and Skar wounded), Matal, Syl.
- *Place:* the western staging plateau, by the chasm, the bridge on their shoulders.
- *Stakes:* the betrayal weighs on him as heavily as the bridge, and he sees the world as rotten.
- *Slice:* **E**. An interior mood piece. Hard to make playable, but it sets the emotional low.

**10. The escape plan (Kaladin POV).**
- *Present:* Bridge Four, Matal (who lets them lag behind).
- *Place:* the chasm edge, army leaving.
- *Stakes:* a real chance at freedom (Kaladin tells the crew they're free). Kaladin plans to stay behind to cover for the wounded in camp, and the crew says they won't leave without him.
- *Slice:* **E**. A quiet group scene with a real choice. A dialogue slice.

**11. Syl and the decision (Kaladin POV).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, Syl (in a full-size human form for the first time, hands clasped, horrified), Rock, Skar, the crew.
- *Place:* the chasm edge, looking east at the Tower.
- *Stakes:* Kaladin tries to refuse, saying they owe Dalinar nothing. Syl says what she is: an honorspren, a spirit of oaths and promises. Flashes of Dallet, Amaram, and his father's voice (someone has to be the first to step forward and do what is right) bring him round. He says they have to go back. Bridge Four nods one by one. He inhales Stormlight, orders the bridge up, and leads the charge.
- *Kaladin:* breathes in a wave of Stormlight to start the charge.
- *Slice:* **E/C**. The moral turning point. How a spren looks when she stands full-size is a standalone visual question. It is also the one scene in the chapter where the decision, not the power, is the climax.

**12. Dalinar sees the bridge crew (Dalinar POV).**
- *Present:* Dalinar, Adolin, remaining Cobalt Guard.
- *Place:* high on the Tower slope looking west.
- *Stakes:* a tiny hope. They form assault formations, fewer than half of the original eight thousand left.
- *Slice:* **C**. A short cutaway that sells the sight of one bridge crew, bridge on their shoulders, running toward the Tower alone.

**13. The arrow volley (Kaladin POV).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, Syl, Bridge Four, Parshendi archers on the Tower side and on the other staging plateau.
- *Place:* the rocky ground between the staging plateau and the chasm.
- *Stakes:* a flanking group of Parshendi archers is about to shoot the crew's unprotected side. Kaladin shouts the order for a side carry to the right, and the crew drops the bridge sideways as a shield. A second, larger volley then comes from behind.
- *Kaladin:* he throws every bit of his Stormlight into the shield with a scream. The arrows are pulled into it, over a hundred of them, and he is thrown back. Moash says it looked as if a sun had burst out of him. The Parshendi archers call out *Neshua Kadal* to one another, in a language Kaladin doesn't understand, and flee. The shield shatters and Kaladin is drained.
- *Slice:* **S/P**. The cleanest standalone set piece in the sequence. Directing a crew with a single shouted command, the shield absorbing a volley, and the aftermath of an emptied hero.

**14. Collapse (Kaladin POV).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, Teft, Lopen, Skar, Dabbid, Syl. The rest of the crew pushes the bridge across.
- *Place:* a hollow on the plateau.
- *Stakes:* Kaladin can barely stand. Teft, Skar (both wounded), one-armed Lopen and Dabbid (who hasn't spoken since being wounded) go to help the crew. Kaladin asks Syl for anything that would make him stronger. She cannot give it.
- *Slice:* **E**. The "hero is out of power and others step up" beat.

**15. Tien (Kaladin POV, memory).**
- *Present (memory):* a younger Kaladin (new to Amaram's army), Dalar, Mesh and Varth (squadleaders), Tien and two other messenger boys, Amaram's cavalry.
- *Place:* the hillside battlefield.
- *Stakes:* Kaladin searches for Tien, is stabbed, kills a man for the first time, and finds Tien dead in front of him. Varth's squad had put the untrained boys in the front line. He holds the body until it is cold, promising Tien he will protect him and bring him home.
- *Slice:* **E**. This is the emotional root of the Second Ideal and the longest single beat in the chapter. It is also a clean test of how a game shows a memory: staged differently from the present, no power, a small boy.

**16. The leap and the Second Ideal (Kaladin POV, with Teft's view of the aftermath).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, Syl, Bridge Four (Teft, Rock, Moash), Parshendi at the chasm.
- *Place:* the bridge, part-way across the chasm, and the far ledge.
- *Stakes:* the memory ends, and Kaladin hears Syl ask whether he knows the Words He rises, takes a spear from Lopen's litter, runs onto the bridge while the crew is still pushing it, and jumps off the end into the Parshendi.
- *Kaladin:* he is drained at the start. Mid-air, he sees the Parshendi's beards woven with glowing gemstones, breathes in, and **Stormlight streams out of the gems toward him** in visible spirals. He lands in a crouch with a ring of Stormlight, his arm wounds closing. He then says the Second Ideal: "I will protect those who cannot protect themselves." A thunderclap with no cloud. A wave of white light throws back the first rank. Moash whispers that something important has just changed.
- *Slice:* **S/C/E/P**. The demo's own scene. Several separate questions live inside it:
  - what the Stormlight-from-gems moment looks like;
  - what the "crack" sounds like;
  - how the crew sees it from behind;
  - what a recharge mechanic would feel like.

### Ch. 68 "Eshonai"

**17. Bridge Four holds the bridgehead (Adolin POV, then Teft POV).**
- *Present:* Adolin and Dalinar's army breaking through; Teft and Bridge Four watching Kaladin fight.
- *Place:* the Tower slope heading downhill; the far end of the bridge.
- *Stakes:* Teft sees that Stormlight does not give skill, it perfects what is there. The crew reorganises around Kaladin (teams to the left and right, the rest holding a defensive formation just to stay alive and keep the Parshendi back). Lopen throws Kaladin fresh spears.
- *Kaladin:* white Stormlight streams off him, precision strikes, speed that Teft sees as barely human.
- *Slice:* **P/S**. Combat as a team: a hero in front and a crew shaping the space. Also the book's clearest description of what Stormlight actually does.

**18. Dalinar's breakout and the Shardbearer duel (Dalinar POV).**
- *Present:* Dalinar, Adolin, Cobalt Guard, a seven-foot Parshendi Shardbearer in silver Plate, Gallant (Dalinar's horse).
- *Place:* the lower Tower slope, near a large book-shaped rock.
- *Stakes:* the Parshendi Shardbearer has waited until escape is possible. Dalinar's Plate is nearly spent. He uses the rock under the enemy's feet as a weapon, loses a gauntlet, is knocked down, and hears the Shardbearer say, in accented Alethi, that it really is him and that he has found him at last.
- *Slice:* **S/P**. A well-staged Shardblade duel with terrain use (cutting the ledge from beneath the enemy), failing armor, and a limit-case for "how does Plate feel when it's out of Light".

**19. The bridgehead fight and the Parshendi (Kaladin POV).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, Bridge Four, Syl (spinning around him), Parshendi fighting in pairs.
- *Place:* the Tower side of the chasm, next to the bridge.
- *Stakes:* Kaladin feels fully alive. He notices the Parshendi fight carefully, avoid the wounded, and seem to respect him. He is struck that something so terrible can be so beautiful at once. Later Parshendi stop wearing gems, which looks like they have worked out what he is doing.
- *Kaladin:* Stormlight fuel comes from gems in the enemies' beards. As that fuel dries up, he realises how timely the Alethi's arrival was. The cost of overuse is a worse crash afterward.
- *Slice:* **P/E**. A resource loop (feed on the enemy's gems, then get starved) plus a moral note. Three of the crew die: Malop, Earless Jaks and Narm.

**20. Kaladin takes command (Kaladin POV).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, Moash, Nacomb Gaval (a young lighteyes, field-promoted), the Cobalt Guard, Adolin.
- *Place:* the bridgehead and the mass of wounded soldiers.
- *Stakes:* a darkeyed slave orders lighteyes officers around because someone must. He then shouts Adolin out of a fight, tells him to hold the bridge but only the bridge, and says he will go for Dalinar. He sends Moash with the Cobalt Guard.
- *Slice:* **E/P**. A triage-and-command scene that could be a playable "who do you order where" moment.

**21. The rescue (Kaladin POV, then Dalinar POV).**
- *Present:* Kaladin, Dalinar (down), the Parshendi Shardbearer, ring of Parshendi watchers, Gallant.
- *Place:* the ring of ground below the rock ledge.
- *Stakes:* Kaladin stabs his spear twice into the crack in the Shardbearer's leg Plate, the move he used to save Amaram. The Parshendi begin to whisper *Neshua Kadal* again and sing a new song. He hides his glow, fetches Gallant and bluntly orders Dalinar onto the horse.
- *Slice:* **P/C**. A short, precise "target the crack in the Plate" encounter, and the first real meeting of Kaladin and Dalinar, in a lighteyes–darkeyes power reversal.

**22. The retreat and the salute (Dalinar POV).**
- *Present:* Dalinar, Kaladin, Adolin, the army, the Parshendi Shardbearer.
- *Place:* across the bridge and on the near plateau.
- *Stakes:* the escape feels like more of a victory than any gemheart. The wounded Parshendi Shardbearer raises his Blade in a salute across the chasm, and Dalinar salutes back after a ten-heartbeat pause. The bridge is pulled over.
- *Slice:* **C/E**. A quiet enemy-to-enemy moment. A strong cinematic closing shot.

**23. The talk (Dalinar POV).**
- *Present:* Dalinar, Kaladin (treating a wounded man), Bridge Four.
- *Place:* the plateau after the retreat.
- *Stakes:* Dalinar sees the slave brands. The crew's leader says Sadeas did not send them back; they came on their own, against Sadeas's wishes. Dalinar offers to take them to his camp and vows they will be safe, on all the honor he has. Kaladin agrees, mainly for the wounded back in camp. Dalinar knows he cannot go to war with Sadeas yet.
- *Slice:* **E**. The dialogue exchange the whole sequence is built toward. A good candidate for how a conversation scene plays as a game.

**After (not in scope):** ch. 69 "Justice" opens in Sadeas's warcamp with Navani, and the aftermath unfolds there.

## Lore flags (places the map, brief or call may be off)

1. **"The bridge stalls halfway across the chasm"** (map Destination). The text is close but not identical. The crew is **still pushing** the bridge across when Kaladin runs onto it, and it was out over the chasm but had not yet reached the far side (ch. 67). They are not stuck. Teft sets it down just after he lands. Worth keeping if "stalls" is B's own phrase, but the book's picture is a bridge in motion.
2. **The leap needs one extra fact.** Kaladin is **drained** when he leaps. The Stormlight arrives mid-air, drawn from the **gems in the Parshendi's beards** (ch. 67). That is the visual hook, and it reads differently from "he leaps and glows". He also **speaks the Second Ideal after landing**, not mid-air.
3. **Lashings.** The brief says that in book 1 "he uses Stormlight and Lashings" (brief, lore corrections). In chs. 65–68 I found **no Lashing** and no named Surge. The leap is a human jump boosted by Stormlight. The shield volley is the only unexplained power move. The text calls it neither Adhesion nor a Lashing (ch. 67). The closest moments in the run-up are in ch. 59: stones fixed to a wall by Stormlight (it looks like Adhesion, but the text doesn't name it) and a failed attempt to run up a wall. Treat the shield volley as unlabelled.
4. **Syl at the Third Ideal, checked.** The brief says Syl becomes a Blade at the Third Ideal in book 2. That holds: Kaladin speaks "I will protect even those I hate. So long as it is right" in *Words of Radiance* ch. 84, and Syl first forms into the Blade in ch. 86. So in ch. 67 she doesn't become a weapon. She appears human-sized and says what she is.
5. **"Eshonai" (ch. 68's title).** The chapter text never names the Parshendi Shardbearer. The title is the only pointer, so don't treat the name as in-scene knowledge for Dalinar or Kaladin.
6. **The brief says the danger on bridge runs is archers.** Confirmed: in chs. 65–68 the threats are archers, slingers, swordsmen, hammers, and one Shardbearer. No monsters appear. A chasmfiend is only the prize: its chrysalis and gemheart are what the armies are fighting over.
7. **Not lore, but a scope note.** The map says the demo is ch. 67. Most of what makes the Second Ideal land is *inside* ch. 67 (beats 9–15, especially Tien), and the payoff fight and Dalinar's rescue are in ch. 68. A demo that stops at the Ideal leaves out the "it changed something" proof.

## Strongest slice candidates (my suggestions, not B's)

These are mine. B and A have not weighed in. Ordered loosely by how cleanly each stands alone.

1. **The gem-drain leap (beat 16).** A single, self-contained moment: the leap off the bridge, Stormlight streaming out of beard gems, the landing ring, the Second Ideal, the thunderclap. It is the demo's centre and also a pure visual/sound question.
2. **Side carry right and the arrow volley (beat 13).** A crew command, a shield volley, an emptied hero. It is the best-contained playable-feeling spectacle and works without the rest of the plot.
3. **Syl at full size (beat 11).** How a spren looks when she stops being a ribbon of light, and her explanation of what she is. A character-design slice that doesn't need combat.
4. **Tien in the hollow (beat 15).** The emotional root. A slice about *how a memory plays as a game*: the staging, the sound, the absence of power.
5. **The decoy dance (beat 2).** A rhythm-style dodge with a hidden-resource twist (don't glow too much). It is the one place where the book describes Kaladin's movement as a flow.
6. **Gems as fuel in the bridgehead fight (beats 13, 17, 19).** A resource mechanic: draw Stormlight from enemy gems, then have the enemy learn to leave them behind (ch. 68). It touches the open combat-model questions on the map without picking a side.
7. **The Shardbearer duel (beat 18).** The Plate-leaks-Light framing is distinct from Kaladin's fight and could be a contrasting combat slice.
8. **Dalinar's rescue, with Kaladin ordering him onto his horse (beat 21).** A small, precise "hit the crack in the Plate" moment that also holds the first Kaladin–Dalinar exchange.
9. **The salute across the chasm (beat 22).** A closing cinematic image, cheap to explore as a still.

Two non-obvious things I'd flag for B: the Parshendi come out of this chapter as respected opponents (beat 19), which may matter for how enemies are designed. And beats 5 and 8 (Dalinar's doubts) are what give Kaladin's choice its contrast, so a demo that follows only Kaladin loses some of it.
